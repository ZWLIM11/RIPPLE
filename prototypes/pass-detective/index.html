How the model ranks passes

Pure calculation module: PassingModel.recommend(state). No network calls, trained model, or language model is used in this prototype.

For each candidate, estimate foot-dependent preparation, ball travel with deceleration, receiver arrival/control, defender arrival along the route (samples at most 1 m apart), and pressure before release. Arrival assumes reaction, turning, acceleration, and a capped running speed. Interception margin is defender arrival minus ball arrival from the same decision moment.

Keep possession: 75% normalized safety + 25% execution ease. Progress the attack: 45% safety + 30% normalized forward progression + 15% space after reception + 10% execution ease, minus a central/deep turnover penalty. These weights and 0–100 scores are experimental heuristics, not completion probabilities. A viable option needs >0.08 s route margin, >0.10 s receiver-control margin, and >0.05 s release margin.

“Similar options” appears when top distinct receivers are within five score points or the best receiver changes under ±10% ball-speed and ±0.1 s reaction variations. This sensitivity check is not a confidence interval. The offside filter checks attacking-half position against the ball and second-last opponent at release; a full offside offence requires later involvement and is outside this snapshot model.

Limitations: stationary starting velocities, simplified reaction/turning and foot geometry, sampled straight ground passes, no shielding, tackling interactions, curved or lofted passes, or calibrated probabilities. Teammate obstruction is not modeled. “Best” means best among this finite set under these assumptions. A short pass that needs no kick adjustment may still be tactically preferable in ways the score misses.

PROTOTYPE · In-memory state only. Reset or reload restores a scenario. Change the board to challenge the recommendation; the prototype has not yet validated the model.
